# Known risks

Written before the first line of app code, most severe first.

1. The voice does not sound like a duck, and there is no ground truth to tune against. Pollen's sound bank lives on the robot's filesystem; we have never heard it, and we have not verified whether the WAVs are in the Apache-2.0 repo or redistributable. A procedural synth judged by one person's ear is the honest fallback, but 'sounds cheap' is exactly the failure that turns a charming free toy into a one-star novelty, and no unit test can catch it. Mitigation: this is the FIRST session, before any AR work, with an explicit human ear gate; M0-1 checks the licence in twenty minutes; if the answer is no, open an issue with Pollen. Ship-silent is a real option and a bad one, so it is the third choice, not the first.

2. App Store Guideline 4.2, Minimum Functionality. A soundboard is close to the canonical example Apple rejects, and 'seven buttons that play sounds' is what the app looks like in a screenshot. Mitigation is substance, not argument: the AR performance driven by a real ONNX policy, the pinch-to-scale actual-size duck, the petting interaction, the App Intents/widget in M2, and the robot-control feature in M3. The review notes should say plainly that the duck is driven by a trained reinforcement-learning policy running on-device at 50 Hz and that the app is a preview for a shipping robot. Assume at least one rejection round and do not schedule around a clean first pass.

3. 'Duck Sounds' is one of the most crowded names on the App Store — hunting calls, kids' animal apps, ad-laden soundboards, dozens of them. A free, clean, no-ads app will not out-rank them on that keyword, which puts the 1,000-download PASS gate directly at risk for a reason that has nothing to do with the product. Mitigation: subtitle and keywords target 'robot duck', 'AR duck', 'duck call' rather than fighting 'duck sounds'; the GATES middle case already pre-registers a listing rewrite at day 120. Consider a more distinctive App Store display name before M1-19, while the repo name stays duckbench.

4. The standing policy may barely track head commands, which would make four of the seven performances limp. The head rides in the 13-value command block and is deliberately NOT added on top of the policy output (doing both bends the head twice), so how much neck_pitch and head_roll excursion alpha_stand actually produces for a commanded step is an unknown we have not measured. inquire is a head tilt and peck is a head dive; if the policy damps them, they read as nothing. Mitigation: M1-3 is a measurement on the Pi before the timelines are written, and the fallback (compose policy legs with direct-driven head joints, filtered at the trained alpha=0.5 and clamped to travel) is designed but not built.

5. The duck has no geometry. robot_walk.xml has 14 hinges, sites, inertial frames and ZERO geom elements, so there is nothing upstream to render. Fifteen primitives placed at exact MJCF frames is correct kinematically and may still look like fifteen boxes rather than a duck. Charm is the product; 'accurate but ugly' fails the same way 'bad audio' does. Mitigation: sizes come from each body's inertial mass and centre offset rather than guesses, the beak and eyes get hand attention, and the render is swappable if Pollen's meshes turn out to be usable — the pose pipeline does not change either way.

6. AR cannot be tested where the code is built. The Simulator has no ARKit, the operator has no local Mac, and mac_build.py only proves it compiles. Every AR behaviour — plane detection on a carpet, scale reading correctly at 25 cm, the duck not sliding, drag-steering feeling right — is only knowable on a physical iPhone via TestFlight. Mitigation: the non-AR fallback scene is built FIRST so the simulator build exercises the real render path, and M1-18 is an explicit device smoke step, not an afterthought. Budget an extra TestFlight round trip for AR bugs that only appear on hardware.

7. The socat bridge is unauthenticated on the LAN. Anyone on the Wi-Fi can drive a $399 robot that walks. We are publishing the instruction, which makes us partly responsible for the consequence. Mitigation: pairing off by default, an explicit warning before first connect, the wheee-does-not-walk-a-real-duck rule with a separate opt-in override, and the ssh -L alternative documented for people who want it done properly. This is M3, so there is time to reconsider — but 'a phone cannot open a Unix socket on another machine' does not go away, and any better answer means asking Pollen to ship a TCP listener.

8. The 60- and 90-day gates land almost exactly on top of first hardware deliveries. Ship in early September, and day 60 is mid-November and day 90 is mid-December — right when duck owners start appearing and the app's whole M3 half becomes evaluable. A KILL fired at day 90 could kill the app three weeks before the audience it was built for exists. Mitigation: the hardware gate runs on its own clock from delivery, and the day-90 KILL is explicitly about the CONSUMER thesis (a soundboard nobody reopens), not the robot thesis. If the day-90 numbers are borderline, note it and hold to the pre-registered day-120 rescue rather than inventing a new reason to keep going.

9. Trademark and goodwill. 'Microduck' is Pollen's product name and the app trades on their robot's likeness, sounds and trained weights. The weights and model are Apache-2.0 and the name is used nominatively, so the legal risk is low, but the relationship risk is not: an app that misrepresents their robot's voice or embarrasses it is worse for them than no app. Mitigation: a not-affiliated line in the README, the About screen and the App Store description; the synthesized voice labelled in-app as our impression rather than theirs; and a pre-registered 7-day pull if they ask.

10. Scope creep dressed as charm. Mood, Calls, widgets, App Intents, share links, teach-me-your-voice, a web call player — every one of these is genuinely a good idea, and shipping them in M1 turns a weekend into a month, which changes the economics of the whole bet. The app is justified by costing a weekend. Mitigation: M1 is frozen at the list in PLAN.md; everything else waits for the day-60 gate to say the audience exists.
